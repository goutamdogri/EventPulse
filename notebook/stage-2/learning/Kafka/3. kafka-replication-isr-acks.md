# Kafka: Replication, ISR, `min.insync.replicas` and `acks`

_Learning note 3 of the Stage 2 Kafka series. Read notes 1 (topic, partition, log, segment) and 2 (offsets) first. This note is the foundation for Step 1 (what "202 after Kafka acks" really promises) and Step 5 (Kafka unavailable: 503, `Retry-After`)._

**How much of this was run.** Everything marked **[run]** was executed on this repo's 3-broker cluster and the output is real, including the broker-failure labs 4 to 8 (stop one follower, `min.insync.replicas=3`, stop the leader, stop two brokers, `stop` vs `kill`). Timings are from one run each on a laptop; treat them as the right order of magnitude, not as constants. Where a claim is my reasoning rather than something directly observed, it is labelled **[reasoning]**.

**Three results that changed what I first wrote** (details in sections 10.1, 12 and Labs 5 to 8):

1. **The producer never showed `NOT_ENOUGH_REPLICAS`.** It is a _retriable_ error, so the client retries it silently until the delivery timeout and then reports a **`TimeoutException`**. In practice, "writes are refused" looks like "writes time out" to your code.
2. **Two brokers down gave the same timeout**, and admin commands (`--describe`, quorum status) also hung, because this combined-mode cluster loses its KRaft quorum together with the brokers.
3. **A stopped broker leaves the ISR in about 3 seconds; a killed one takes about 9 seconds** (the broker session timeout), not "up to 30 s".

## 1. TL;DR

- Every partition has **RF copies** (replicas) on different brokers (RF = 3 for us). One is the **leader**; the rest are **followers**. **Producers and consumers talk only to the leader.** Followers just copy the leader's log by **fetching** from it, exactly like a consumer would.
- The **ISR (in-sync replicas)** is the set of replicas, always including the leader, that are **caught up** with the leader. It is the set that is trusted to hold every acknowledged write and the set a new leader may be chosen from.
- The **high watermark (HW)** is the offset up to which **every ISR member** has the data. **Consumers can read only below the HW**, so they never see data that could disappear in a failover.
- Producer **`acks`** decides **who must confirm** before the producer is told "success": `0` nobody, `1` the leader, `all` every member of the current ISR.
- **`min.insync.replicas`** (min ISR) decides the **smallest ISR size at which `acks=all` writes are still accepted**. Below it, the leader **refuses** the write with `NOT_ENOUGH_REPLICAS` instead of acknowledging it with too few copies.
- **Together:** with RF 3, min ISR 2, `acks=all`, a write is acknowledged only when **at least 2 brokers have it**. One broker can be down and writes still work. Two down and writes are refused. That is the EventPulse setting, and it is why Step 1's "202 means durably appended" is a true statement and why Step 5's 503 exists.
- `min.insync.replicas` has **no effect** on `acks=1` or `acks=0`. Weak `acks` bypasses the safety net entirely.

> **One-line version:** _`acks` says who must say yes; `min.insync.replicas` says how many must exist to ask; the ISR is who is currently allowed to say yes._

## 2. The mental model

Imagine a legal contract kept as **three copies in three offices**. One office is the **lead office** (the leader). Every change is made there first, and the other two offices (followers) keep telephoning the lead office to ask "anything new?" and copy it.

| Contract copies                                                         | Kafka                                  |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| The lead office, where all changes are made                             | The partition **leader**               |
| The two other offices that copy it                                      | **Followers**                          |
| Offices that are keeping up and not lagging                             | The **ISR**                            |
| "A change counts as official once every keeping-up office has it"       | The **high watermark**                 |
| "I'll tell the client it is filed only once N offices confirm"          | `acks` and `min.insync.replicas`       |
| "If fewer than 2 offices are keeping up, we stop accepting new filings" | `NOT_ENOUGH_REPLICAS`                  |
| The lead office burns down; a keeping-up office becomes the lead        | Leader election from the ISR           |
| An office that was behind is **never** made the lead office             | `unclean.leader.election.enable=false` |

The central trade-off is simple: **durability against availability.** Insisting on more copies before confirming makes data safer and writes less available. EventPulse chooses safety: it prefers refusing a write (a 503 the client can retry) to confirming one that exists on a single machine.

## 3. Why replicate

Without replication, a partition lives on one broker's disk. If that machine dies, the partition's data is gone and the partition is unavailable. With RF 3:

- **Durability:** the data exists on three machines. As long as one in-sync copy survives, an acknowledged write survives.
- **Availability:** when the leader dies, a follower takes over and clients carry on after a short blip.

Replication is **per partition**, not per topic or per broker. Each of the 6 partitions of `events.raw` has its own leader, its own followers and its own ISR. One broker failing triggers an **independent** election for each partition it led (note 1, section 12). A topic can therefore be "healthy" for some partitions and degraded for others.

Replication is not a backup: a bad write or a deleted topic replicates just as faithfully. It protects against **machine failure**, not against mistakes.

## 4. Leader and followers

Real output from this cluster:

```
Topic: events.raw  PartitionCount: 6  ReplicationFactor: 3  Configs: min.insync.replicas=2,...
  Partition: 0  Leader: 3  Replicas: 3,1,2  Isr: 3,1,2
  Partition: 1  Leader: 1  Replicas: 1,2,3  Isr: 1,2,3
  Partition: 2  Leader: 2  Replicas: 2,3,1  Isr: 2,3,1
```

- **Leader:** the single replica that accepts all produce requests and serves all fetches for that partition. Offsets are assigned here.
- **Followers:** replicas that continuously fetch from the leader and append to their own log. They accept no client traffic by default. (Kafka can allow consumers to fetch from a nearby follower, "follower fetching", but it is off by default and we do not use it.)
- **Replicas:** the assigned list. The first entry is the **preferred leader**, the one the cluster tries to return leadership to so that leaders stay evenly spread. Note: in the output above, broker 3 is the preferred leader of partition 0 and also its current leader.
- **Leader balance:** after a failover and restart, leadership can end up skewed. With `auto.leader.rebalance.enable=true` (our default) the controller checks every `leader.imbalance.check.interval.seconds` (**300 s**) and moves leadership back to preferred leaders. You can also trigger it by hand with `kafka-leader-election.sh --election-type preferred`.
- **Leader epoch:** a number that increases every time a partition gets a new leader. It is recorded in the log's `leader-epoch-checkpoint` file as `epoch startOffset` pairs and is how replicas and consumers tell "who was leader when" (section 11). From the lab below: `0 0` means "epoch 0 began at offset 0".

A follower is not a different kind of process. It is the same broker code, running a **fetcher** thread (`num.replica.fetchers=1` per source broker by default) that sends fetch requests to the leader and appends what it receives. Followers wait up to `replica.fetch.wait.max.ms` (500 ms) at the leader when there is nothing new, so replication latency is usually small.

## 5. How followers copy

Each replica has its own **log end offset (LEO)**: the next offset it would write. The leader also tracks every follower's LEO (learned from the follower's fetch requests).

```
partition P, RF 3. Producer sends 3 records, offsets 10, 11, 12.

 leader   LEO=13   [ .. 10 11 12 ]
 foll A   LEO=13   [ .. 10 11 12 ]    <- fetched everything
 foll B   LEO=11   [ .. 10 ]          <- behind (slow disk, GC pause, network)

 ISR = {leader, A, B}   (B is still "in sync" while it is catching up within the lag limit)
 high watermark = min(LEO of all ISR members) = 11
 -> consumers can read up to offset 10 only. Records 11 and 12 are not yet visible.
 -> an acks=all producer is still waiting for these records.

 B fetches 11 and 12  ->  leader sees B's LEO=13  ->  HW moves to 13
 -> consumers can now read 11, 12. The acks=all producer gets its success.
```

Key facts:

- **High watermark = the smallest LEO among ISR members.** It is the "committed" point of the log. (Do not confuse it with a consumer's committed offset in note 2. Same word, different layer.)
- **Consumers can read only below the high watermark**, so a consumer never sees a record that has not reached all ISR replicas. If the leader then died, the record could be lost; hiding it prevents a consumer from observing data that later "un-happens".
- **Followers learn the HW from the leader**, one fetch round later, so a follower's HW trails the leader's by a moment.
- **Logs are byte-for-byte copies.** Offsets are assigned by the leader and replicated as-is, so all replicas of a partition hold identical segments. **[run]** Proof from this cluster: a 2,000-record partition (`lab.copy-0`) with replicas on all three brokers:

  ```
  broker 1: md5 b7ed70993f35b1765d71cfbfc8c124f7  size 1021916
  broker 2: md5 b7ed70993f35b1765d71cfbfc8c124f7  size 1021916
  broker 3: md5 b7ed70993f35b1765d71cfbfc8c124f7  size 1021916
  ```

  The same file, three machines. This is also why any ISR member can become leader and continue as if nothing happened.

- **The HW is persisted** in each broker's `replication-offset-checkpoint` file **[run]**: it contained `lab.copy 0 2000` (topic, partition, high watermark), plus `kafka-log-dirs.sh` showed `offsetLag 0` on all three replicas.
- A follower that is down and restarts **truncates** any suffix that disagrees with the new leader (using leader epochs), then catches up. Followers never keep data the leader does not have.

## 6. The ISR

### 6.1 Definition

The ISR is the set of replicas that are **fully caught up enough to be trusted**: the leader plus every follower that has been keeping up. Formally, a follower is in the ISR as long as it has **fetched up to the leader's LEO at least once within the last `replica.lag.time.max.ms`** (default **30,000 ms**, confirmed on this cluster).

Key properties:

- The ISR **always includes the leader**, so its size is at least 1.
- It is a **dynamic** set: it **shrinks** when a follower falls behind or its broker disappears, and **expands** when a follower catches up again.
- A follower that is slow but within the lag limit stays in the ISR, and then **it slows `acks=all` writes**, because the HW cannot advance past it (section 14).

### 6.2 Who changes it

- The **leader** notices a lagging follower and asks the **controller** (the KRaft quorum) to remove it (an `AlterPartition` request). When the follower catches up, the leader asks to add it back.
- The **controller** also removes a broker's replicas from ISRs directly when the broker is **fenced**: it missed heartbeats for `broker.session.timeout.ms` (**9,000 ms** here), or it did a controlled shutdown (`docker stop` sends SIGTERM, which triggers a graceful, faster path).
- The ISR is stored in the **cluster metadata log**, so it survives restarts, and `kafka-topics.sh --describe` reads it from there.

### 6.3 Why the ISR matters, in one sentence each

- **For writes:** `acks=all` waits for the **whole current ISR**, not for "all replicas". A dead follower that has already been removed from the ISR no longer blocks writes. (Until it is removed, which can take up to ~30 s for a hung but connected follower, writes wait on it or time out.)
- **For safety:** only an ISR member may be elected leader, because only ISR members are guaranteed to hold every acknowledged record.
- **For reading:** the HW is computed over the ISR.

### 6.4 A newer detail you will see

`--describe` on Kafka 4.x also prints `Elr` and `LastKnownElr` columns (eligible leader replicas). They are empty when all is healthy. They exist to make failure cases around min ISR safer and you do not need them for this stage. Ignore them for now.

## 7. Producer `acks`

`acks` is a **producer** setting (`KAFKA_ACKS` in EventPulse, default `-1` = `all`). It decides when the producer is told a send succeeded.

| `acks`           | The leader responds when...                                                                                                    | Survives leader crash right after ack?                                                                                                     | Uses `min.insync.replicas`? |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------- |
| **`0`**          | **never waits**; the producer does not even wait for a response                                                                | No guarantee. The producer does not know if the leader got it. Errors are invisible                                                        | No                          |
| **`1`**          | the leader has **appended to its own log** (OS page cache)                                                                     | **No.** If the leader dies before a follower copied it, the record is lost; after the election the old leader truncates it when it returns | No                          |
| **`all`** (`-1`) | **every replica in the current ISR** has the record (HW passed it), **and** the ISR had at least `min.insync.replicas` members | **Yes**, as long as at least one ISR member survives. With min ISR 2, at least 2 copies existed at ack time                                | **Yes**                     |

Notes:

- `acks=all` means all replicas **currently in the ISR**, not "all 3 replicas". If the ISR shrank to 2, then "all" means 2. That is exactly why min ISR exists: without it, "all" could silently shrink to just the leader.
- **`acks=all` with `min.insync.replicas=1` is a trap:** the ISR can shrink to the leader alone and `acks=all` then means "the leader has it". It looks safe and is not.
- **Idempotence needs `acks=all`.** `enable.idempotence=true` requires it (our `KAFKA_IDEMPOTENT=true` default forces it, as the plan says).
- **Durability is replication, not fsync** (note 1, section 7.1). "Acknowledged with `acks=all`, min ISR 2" means "in the memory/page cache of at least two brokers", which survives any single machine failure but not a simultaneous power loss of both.
- **[run]** On the healthy cluster, `acks=all`, `acks=1` and `acks=0` all succeed and give similar results for 5 tiny records. The difference only appears under failure (and in latency under load, K2).

### 7.1 What the broker does for `acks=all`

```
 ProduceRequest(acks=all) arrives at the leader
   1. validate; check  ISR size >= min.insync.replicas ?   no -> reject NOT_ENOUGH_REPLICAS (nothing appended)
   2. append to the leader's log, assign offsets
   3. park the request ("delayed produce") until HW >= last offset + 1
        - followers fetch the batch, report their new LEO
        - leader recomputes the HW over the ISR
   4. HW passed the batch -> respond success
      if the ISR shrank below min.insync.replicas while waiting -> NOT_ENOUGH_REPLICAS_AFTER_APPEND
      if nothing happens within the request timeout -> REQUEST_TIMED_OUT
```

Steps 1 and 3 are the two moments where `min.insync.replicas` and the ISR do their work.

## 8. `min.insync.replicas`

It is a **topic config** (with a broker default; ours is 2 at both levels). Its exact meaning:

> When a producer uses `acks=all`, the leader accepts the write only if the ISR currently has **at least `min.insync.replicas`** members. Otherwise it rejects with `NOT_ENOUGH_REPLICAS`.

Three things people get wrong:

1. **It does not mean "write to N replicas".** It means "refuse to acknowledge a write unless the ISR has at least N members". The replication itself is always to the whole ISR.
2. **It does nothing for `acks=1` or `0`.** Those writes are accepted by a leader alone, whatever the ISR size.
3. **It also protects other topics that use `acks=all`.** Our brokers set it as a **default of 2**, so internal topics inherit it. **[run]** `__consumer_offsets` shows `min.insync.replicas=2`. Consequence: **when the ISR of a `__consumer_offsets` partition drops below 2, consumers cannot commit offsets for groups coordinated there** (commits are `acks=all` writes). Group management then misbehaves too. Losing two of three brokers therefore hurts consumers as well as producers (note 2, section 7).

**[run]** A related observation worth knowing: on this version, setting a topic's `min.insync.replicas` **above** its RF (we tried 4 on an RF 3 topic) did **not** make `acks=all` writes fail. All writes succeeded. So you cannot fake the "refusal" with a bad config. The refusal appears only when the **ISR is actually smaller than min ISR**, which means a replica really has to be down or lagging (labs 5 to 8).

### 8.1 Choosing RF and min ISR

The two rules to remember:

```
simultaneous broker failures survived with NO LOSS of acknowledged data   = min.insync.replicas - 1
simultaneous broker failures survived with writes STILL ACCEPTED          = RF - min.insync.replicas
```

(both with `acks=all`). So RF 3 / min ISR 2: lose no data through 1 failure, keep accepting writes through 1 failure. Raise min ISR to 3 and you tolerate 2 failures without data loss, but accept writes through **0** failures. Lower it to 1 and you accept writes through 2 failures but could lose data even on 1.

## 9. The decision table

All rows assume 3 brokers and idempotence off for clarity. "Failures" means brokers down **at the same time** and the surviving ISR.

| RF  | min ISR | acks    | 0 down          | 1 down (ISR=2)  | 2 down (ISR=1)                      | Data safety                                         |
| --- | ------- | ------- | --------------- | --------------- | ----------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| 3   | 2       | **all** | acked, 3 copies | acked, 2 copies | **refused** (`NOT_ENOUGH_REPLICAS`) | no loss through 1 failure. **EventPulse**           |
| 3   | 1       | all     | acked           | acked           | acked with **1 copy**               | loss possible on a single failure after ISR shrinks |
| 3   | 3       | all     | acked           | **refused**     | refused                             | no loss through 2, but any failure stops writes     |
| 3   | any     | **1**   | acked           | acked           | acked                               | leader-only ack. A leader crash can lose acked data |
| 3   | any     | **0**   | "acked"         | "acked"         | "acked"                             | fire and forget; no loss visibility at all          |
| 1   | 1       | all     | acked           | topic offline   | topic offline                       | any failure loses availability and maybe data       |

Reading the EventPulse row: it never acknowledges a write that exists on fewer than 2 machines. When two machines are gone, it chooses to return errors (503 to clients) over acknowledging risky writes. That is the Stage 2 stance, and the audit's check C ("every acknowledged event is in the log") depends on it.

## 10. Errors

### 10.1 What the producer actually reports [run, Labs 5 and 7]

`NOT_ENOUGH_REPLICAS`, `NOT_LEADER_OR_FOLLOWER`, `LEADER_NOT_AVAILABLE` and `REQUEST_TIMED_OUT` are all **retriable**. A producer that sees one does not hand it to your code. It retries inside the library until the **delivery timeout** (`delivery.timeout.ms` for the Java client, `message.timeout.ms` for librdkafka, which our Node client uses) runs out, and only then fails the send. What we observed with the Java perf-test client, `delivery.timeout.ms=9000`:

```
ISR = 2, min ISR = 3, acks=all:
  org.apache.kafka.common.errors.TimeoutException: Expiring 5 record(s) for lab.repl-0:9000 ms has passed
  since batch creation. The request has not been sent, or no server response has been received yet
  -> 0 records sent, total time 11.9 s
```

So the failure you handle in Step 5 is, in practice, **"the send timed out after the delivery timeout"**, whatever broker-side reason caused it (ISR too small, no leader, broker unreachable, quorum lost). The reason is mostly hidden from the caller. For the Node client **[reasoning]** the final error should be librdkafka's message-timed-out code, possibly with the last underlying error attached; check what your client really returns by making the same failure happen (Lab 5) and logging the whole error object. Design consequences:

- **Latency of a failed request = the delivery timeout**, not instant. This is why the batcher's send timeout (`KAFKA_SEND_TIMEOUT_MS`) and the circuit breaker in Step 5 are needed: without them every request during an outage waits the full timeout.
- **Do not set the producer's delivery timeout longer than your HTTP request budget.** Keep it below `KAFKA_SEND_TIMEOUT_MS` or the two will fight.
- `acks=1` and `acks=0` writes **succeeded** in the same state (Lab 5), which is the clearest demonstration of why they are unsafe.

Two lessons for **Step 5 (`isKafkaUnavailable`)**:

- The mapping must include the **timeout** errors as well as `NOT_ENOUGH_REPLICAS`. Several different broker states reach the HTTP layer as "Kafka unavailable".
- Failures with a **"maybe appended"** outcome are why a client retrying the same `event_id` can create a duplicate in Kafka, which the sink's `ON CONFLICT (event_id)` then absorbs. That is the known Stage 2 ledger item ("a rejected or timed-out batch may still have been written").

## 11. Broker failure

### 11.1 What happens when a leader's broker dies

```
 t0   broker 1 (leader of P, ISR {1,2,3}) dies
 t1   controller notices (heartbeat timeout up to 9 s, or instantly on controlled shutdown)
 t2   controller picks a new leader from the ISR (the first live ISR member in the replicas order)
        -> leader epoch increases, ISR shrinks to {2,3}, metadata log records both
 t3   brokers apply the metadata; broker 2 starts serving P as leader
 t4   clients that sent to broker 1 get errors / timeouts, refresh metadata, find broker 2, retry
```

- **Window of unavailability:** from the failure until a client learns the new leader. With a graceful stop it is typically sub-second to a few seconds; with a hard kill it also includes the detection delay (up to about 9 s here). During that time `acks=all` writes to that partition fail or time out and are retried.
- **No acknowledged `acks=all` write is lost**, because the new leader came from the ISR: it had every record the old leader acknowledged (min ISR 2 guaranteed at least one such surviving copy).
- **Un-acknowledged data may or may not survive.** Records the old leader had appended but not yet replicated may be gone after the election. The producer never got an ack for them, so a retry covers it. This is the "maybe" zone in section 10.
- If **no ISR member is alive**, the partition goes **offline**. With `unclean.leader.election.enable=false` (ours), Kafka **waits** for an ISR member to return instead of electing an out-of-sync replica that would lose acknowledged data. It prefers unavailability to data loss.
- **Restarted replicas rejoin as followers**: they truncate to match the new leader's log (guided by leader epochs), catch up, and rejoin the ISR. Leadership returns to the preferred leader at the next imbalance check (up to 300 s) or on a manual preferred election.

### 11.2 Scenarios for EventPulse (RF 3, min ISR 2, `acks=all`, 6 partitions, 3 brokers)

| Scenario                                    | ISR of affected partitions                                                                                          | What clients see                                                                                         | Data                                                                                                   |
| ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Healthy                                     | 3 of 3                                                                                                              | 202                                                                                                      | 3 copies                                                                                               |
| **One follower broker down**                | shrinks to 2 once the broker is removed (up to `replica.lag.time.max.ms` for a hung one, quickly for a stopped one) | Possibly a brief delay while writes wait on the dead follower, then normal 202s                          | 2 copies. Still acknowledged safely                                                                    |
| **One broker down, it led some partitions** | those partitions elect a new leader; ISR 2                                                                          | A blip of errors/retries on those partitions (a few seconds), then 202s                                  | No acked loss                                                                                          |
| **Broker restarts** (rolling restart)       | rejoin the ISR after catching up                                                                                    | Mostly invisible if brokers are restarted **one at a time and the ISR is allowed to recover in between** | No loss. Restarting a second broker before the first has rejoined the ISR can drop ISRs below 2        |
| **Two brokers down**                        | down to 1 **[see section 12]**                                                                                      | **Refused or timed out**: never a 202                                                                    | No new data accepted; old data safe on the survivor                                                    |
| **All three down**                          | partition offline                                                                                                   | all sends fail                                                                                           | Data is on disk. On restart, ISR members return and the partitions come back (unclean election is off) |
| **A follower is slow, not dead**            | stays in the ISR until it lags past 30 s                                                                            | `acks=all` latency rises to the slowest follower; then the follower is removed and latency recovers      | Safe                                                                                                   |

## 12. The KRaft quorum caveat

This cluster runs **combined mode**: each of the 3 nodes is **both a broker and a KRaft controller**. The controller quorum (the metadata log) needs a **majority**: 2 of 3 voters. The ISR and leader information live in that metadata log, and **every ISR change and every election goes through the active controller**.

**Observed [run, Lab 7].** With brokers 2 and 3 stopped and broker 1 (the leader of the test partition) still up:

- `kafka-topics.sh --describe` and `kafka-metadata-quorum.sh describe --status` against broker 1 **returned nothing within 25 s** (they hung). Admin tooling stops working.
- A producer with `acks=all` **timed out after the 9 s delivery timeout** (13 s total) with the same `TimeoutException: Expiring 5 record(s)...` as in Lab 5, and **nothing was appended**: the partition's end offset did not move.
- A producer with `acks=1` **succeeded** against the same broker.
- After restarting both brokers the cluster was fully replicated in **11 s** and no acknowledged data was lost.

**Why [reasoning, consistent with the above].** Two nodes down means the controller quorum is **also** lost (1 voter of 3 is not a majority). With no active controller nobody can shrink the ISR or elect a leader, so the surviving broker cannot complete `acks=all` writes and the metadata/admin paths hang. The exact internal reason `acks=all` is not even appended was not inspected; what matters is the observable result, which is a timeout.

What this means:

- The Step 5 plan row "stop 2 of 3 brokers: `NOT_ENOUGH_REPLICAS`, 503, no 202" **holds as "503, never 202"**, but the error you will actually see is a **timeout**, taking up to the send timeout until the circuit breaker opens. That is why the plan has `KAFKA_SEND_TIMEOUT_MS` and the breaker, and why `isKafkaUnavailable` must map timeouts as well. Change the e2e assertion from "NOT_ENOUGH_REPLICAS" to "503 with `reason: kafka_unavailable`".
- Even in the cleaner case (**one follower lost, quorum healthy, ISR below min ISR**, Lab 5) the client still reports a timeout, not `NOT_ENOUGH_REPLICAS` (section 10.1). The difference between the two cases is visible only on the broker side (`--under-min-isr-partitions` works in Lab 5 and hangs in Lab 7).
- It is also a reason production clusters usually run **dedicated controllers** separate from brokers. Combined mode with 3 nodes is a development shape. Note it in the Stage 2 report as a limitation of the test cluster.

## 13. Retries and duplicates

Replication creates the "maybe" outcomes from section 10, and retries turn "maybe" into possible duplicates:

```
 producer sends batch  -> leader appends (offsets 40..49)
 leader replicates, but the RESPONSE is lost (network, or leader dies before responding)
 producer sees a timeout, retries the same batch
   without idempotence: the batch is appended AGAIN  (offsets 50..59)   <- duplicate in the log
   with idempotence:    leader sees (producerId, sequence) already stored -> returns success, appends nothing
```

- The idempotent producer attaches a **producer id** and a **sequence number** to each batch (you saw `producerId` and `baseSequence` in the note 1 dump). The leader remembers recent sequences and discards a repeat. That state is part of the log, so it survives a leader change.
- This makes **client-library retries** safe. It does **not** protect against **your own application-level retries**: if a client of the HTTP API gets a 503 and posts again, that is a new `producer.send()` with a new sequence, so the record is appended again. The sink dedupes it by `event_id`. That is why the API contract says "retry with the **same `event_id`**".
- The idempotent producer's guarantee holds **within one producer session** to one partition. Restart the API and the producer gets a new id.

## 14. Cost

| Cost                             | Why                                                                                             | Where it shows up                                                                                                   |
| -------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Latency of `acks=all`**        | The ack waits for the **slowest ISR follower**: a network round trip plus the follower's append | `ep_kafka_send_seconds` (K2)                                                                                        |
| **Network and disk x RF**        | Every byte is written RF times and replicated over the network                                  | The broker ceiling you measure with `kafka-producer-perf-test.sh` (Step 1)                                          |
| **Slow follower drags everyone** | The HW cannot pass the slowest ISR member                                                       | A degraded disk on one broker raises p99 for **every** partition replicated to it, until it is removed from the ISR |
| **Smaller or slower batches**    | A batch is acknowledged as a whole                                                              | Interacts with the micro-batcher size (note 1, section 7.3)                                                         |
| **CPU of replication fetchers**  | Followers do fetch loops per source broker                                                      | Minor at our scale                                                                                                  |

`acks=1` removes the follower wait and is faster, with the durability loss in section 7. K2 sweeps this; the cost of `all` vs `1` is what you will measure, not guess.

## 19. Common confusions

1. **"`acks=all` means all 3 replicas have it."** It means all replicas **currently in the ISR**. The ISR can be smaller than 3.
2. **"`min.insync.replicas=2` means writes go to 2 brokers."** It is a floor on ISR size for `acks=all` writes. Replication is to the whole ISR regardless.
3. **"`min.insync.replicas` protects `acks=1` writes."** No. It only applies to `acks=all`.
4. **"RF 3 means I can lose 2 brokers."** You can lose 2 without losing the **data on the survivor**, but with min ISR 2 you cannot **write**, and in our combined-mode cluster you also lose the metadata quorum.
5. **"The ISR is the list of replicas."** The replica list is the assignment; the ISR is the subset that is caught up right now.
6. **"The leader is always the preferred replica."** After failures it may not be; it moves back at the next rebalance check.
7. **"A follower serves reads."** Not by default. Only the leader serves clients.
8. **"Replication is a backup."** It replicates mistakes too.
9. **"Acknowledged means fsynced."** It means in the page cache of the ISR brokers.
10. **"`NOT_ENOUGH_REPLICAS` always means nothing was written."** `NOT_ENOUGH_REPLICAS_AFTER_APPEND` means it **was** appended. Only the plain code is a clean refusal.
11. **"Idempotence makes my HTTP retries safe."** It protects the client library's own retries within one producer session. HTTP-level retries still need the same `event_id` and a deduplicating sink.
12. **"Replica lag and consumer lag are the same thing."** Different layers.
13. **"Setting min ISR above RF will make writes fail so I can test it."** It did not on this version **[run]**.

<summary>Short answers</summary>

1. The leader handles all produce and fetch. Followers fetch from the leader and append to their own logs.
2. LEO is the next offset a replica will write; the high watermark is the minimum LEO over the ISR. Consumers read below the high watermark.
3. A follower stays in while it has caught up to the leader's LEO within `replica.lag.time.max.ms` (30 s here); it is removed when it lags longer or its broker is fenced/shut down; it rejoins when caught up.
4. The leader requests it and the controller (KRaft quorum) commits it; stored in the cluster metadata log.
5. `0`: nothing. `1`: the leader's append. `all`: every current ISR member, with ISR size at least min ISR.
6. At least 2 (leader plus one follower).
7. The ISR can shrink to the leader alone and `all` then means one copy, giving a false sense of safety.
8. min ISR 2: (a) 1, (b) 1. min ISR 3: (a) 2, (b) 0.
9. The first is rejected before appending (clean). The second was appended but the ISR dropped while waiting; it may survive or be truncated, so a retry may duplicate it.
10. Only an ISR member, because only ISR members hold every acknowledged record. `unclean.leader.election.enable=false`.
11. The first attempt was appended but the ack was lost; the retry appends again. The idempotent producer's producer id and sequence let the leader drop the repeat, within one producer session.
12. Cannot: a single broker failure. Can: losing all (at least) 2 replicas that held it at the same time, such as two machines failing at once, or a bug/delete that replicates.
13. Two nodes down also breaks the 3-voter KRaft quorum, so the ISR cannot be shrunk or a leader elected, and the writes wait for followers that never answer. **[run, Lab 7]**: it timed out after the delivery timeout and nothing was appended. The same timeout (not a visible `NOT_ENOUGH_REPLICAS`) also appeared with one follower down and min ISR 3, because the client retries that error until the delivery timeout (section 10.1).
14. The high watermark cannot pass the slowest ISR member, so every `acks=all` write to partitions that follower is in the ISR for waits for it, until it is removed from the ISR.
15. Writes to that partition with `acks=all` are being refused right now; it is the page-worthy condition.

## 21. Cheat sheet

### Glossary

| Term                  | Meaning                                                                     |
| --------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Replica               | One copy of a partition on one broker                                       |
| Leader                | The replica that serves all client reads and writes                         |
| Follower              | A replica that copies the leader by fetching                                |
| ISR                   | Leader plus followers that are caught up (within `replica.lag.time.max.ms`) |
| LEO                   | Log end offset of a replica                                                 |
| High watermark        | Min LEO across the ISR; consumers read below it                             |
| Preferred leader      | First replica in the assignment list                                        |
| Leader epoch          | Counter that increments on each leader change                               |
| `acks`                | Who must confirm before the producer sees success (0, 1, all)               |
| `min.insync.replicas` | Smallest ISR size at which `acks=all` writes are accepted                   |
| Unclean election      | Electing a non-ISR replica; allowed only if enabled (ours: off)             |
| Under-replicated      | ISR smaller than the replica list                                           |
| Under-min-ISR         | ISR smaller than min ISR; `acks=all` writes refused                         |
| Controller            | KRaft quorum member that decides leaders and ISR changes                    |

### Formulas

```
high watermark                         = min(LEO of ISR members)
no-loss failures tolerated (acks=all)  = min.insync.replicas - 1
write-available failures tolerated     = RF - min.insync.replicas
```
