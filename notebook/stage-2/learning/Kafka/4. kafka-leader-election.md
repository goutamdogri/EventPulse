## 1. TL;DR

- A partition has one **leader** at a time. When its broker fails (or is shut down, or is rebalanced away from), the **controller** picks a **new leader** and every client and follower learns about it through metadata. This is **leader election**.
- A **clean** election chooses only from the **ISR** (in-sync replicas), the replicas that hold every committed record. So a record acknowledged under `acks=all` with `min.insync.replicas=2` survives the loss of any single broker.
- Each election increments the partition's **leader epoch**. Followers use epochs to **truncate** any records that disagree with the new leader, and brokers use epochs to **fence** an old leader that does not know it was replaced.
- If **no ISR member** is alive, the partition goes **offline**. Kafka then has a choice: **wait** for an ISR member to come back (consistency, the default), or elect an out-of-sync replica (**unclean election**, availability).
- **Unclean leader election** (`unclean.leader.election.enable`, **off by default** and **off on this cluster**) trades **possible permanent loss of acknowledged data** for **getting the partition writable again**. The data the stale replica never copied is gone, and consumers can be left pointing at offsets that no longer exist or that now hold different records.
- Producers and consumers do not take part. They **retry and refresh metadata**. What the caller sees is a **short blip of retriable errors**, not a failure, provided the election is clean and quick.

> **One-line version:** _Election picks a replacement from the replicas that provably have all committed data; "unclean" means picking one that might not, because a slow or missing partition feels worse than lost records._

## 2. The mental model

Back to the three-office contract from note 3: one lead office and two others that copy it, and a **registry** (the controller) that decides who is the lead office.

| Offices                                                                                               | Kafka                                                        |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| The lead office burns down                                                                            | The leader's broker fails                                    |
| The registry looks at the offices that were fully caught up when the fire started                     | The controller considers the ISR                             |
| It names the first caught-up office, in a fixed order of preference, as the new lead                  | The first live ISR member in the replica list becomes leader |
| Every filing now carries a **version number of who was in charge**                                    | The **leader epoch**                                         |
| A late office compares its copy with the new lead's and discards pages the new lead never had         | Follower **truncation**                                      |
| An old lead office that comes back and tries to file under its old authority is turned away           | **Fencing** by epoch                                         |
| No caught-up office exists; the registry can **wait**, or name an office that is missing recent pages | Offline partition vs **unclean election**                    |

The whole topic is one question: _when the best copy is gone, do you wait for it or settle for a worse one?_

## 3. When and who

### 3.1 What triggers an election

| Trigger                                                      | Notes                                                                                                                                                                       |
| ------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Broker failure**                                           | The controller stops hearing the broker's heartbeats for `broker.session.timeout.ms` (**9,000 ms** here) and **fences** it                                                  |
| **Controlled shutdown**                                      | A broker that is stopped normally tells the controller first; leaders are moved away **before** it goes (`controlled.shutdown.enable=true` here)                            |
| **Preferred-leader rebalance**                               | Automatic (`auto.leader.rebalance.enable=true`, every `leader.imbalance.check.interval.seconds` = 300 s) or by hand: leadership moves back to the first replica in the list |
| **Topic creation**                                           | The initial leader is chosen (here by the first entry in the replica assignment)                                                                                            |
| **Partition reassignment**                                   | Replicas are moved between brokers; leadership may change                                                                                                                   |
| **Operator-requested election**                              | `kafka-leader-election.sh` with `PREFERRED` or `UNCLEAN`                                                                                                                    |
| **ISR change that removes the current leader's eligibility** | Rare; for example the leader is fenced                                                                                                                                      |

### 3.2 Who does it

The **active controller**, a node in the **KRaft quorum** (note 3, section 12). Brokers do not elect among themselves. The controller:

1. decides the new leader,
2. writes the decision as a record in the **cluster metadata log** (leader id, leader epoch, ISR),
3. and brokers, clients and followers learn it by reading or receiving that metadata.

There is no ZooKeeper in this cluster. Because the decision is a record in a replicated log, all brokers agree on it, and a single authority prevents two leaders for one partition.

## 4. Two elections

There are **two separate elections**, and mixing them up causes confusion in failure tests:

|                 | Controller election                                       | Partition leader election                                                |
| --------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| What is elected | The **active controller** among the KRaft voters          | The **leader of one partition** among its replicas                       |
| Mechanism       | Raft: voters vote; majority needed (2 of 3)               | The active controller picks, from the ISR                                |
| Scope           | One per cluster                                           | One per partition (6 for `events.raw`, 50 for `__consumer_offsets`, ...) |
| Needs a quorum  | Yes (it is the quorum)                                    | Needs a **working active controller**                                    |
| Seen with       | `kafka-metadata-quorum.sh describe --status` (`LeaderId`) | `kafka-topics.sh --describe` (`Leader`)                                  |

In this **combined-mode** cluster every node is both a voter and a broker. If the node that is currently the **active controller** also dies, the cluster must first **re-elect a controller** (a Raft election, normally a second or two) and **then** the new controller elects partition leaders. So losing the controller node costs a little more than losing a plain broker. We saw `LeaderId: 3` earlier in the quorum status, so on that run broker 3 was the controller.

If a majority of voters is gone (two of three), **no controller can be elected, so no partition leader can be elected, and the ISR cannot change.** That is the situation behind note 3's Lab 7 result (`acks=all` timed out, admin tools hung, `acks=1` still worked against the surviving leader).

## 5. Clean election

### 5.1 The rule

For a partition whose leader is gone, the controller chooses:

> **the first replica, in the order of the partition's replica list, that is (a) in the ISR, and (b) alive and not shutting down.**

That is why the replica list order matters: it is the **preference order**. In `Replicas: 3,1,2` broker 3 is the preferred leader; if it dies, broker 1 is tried next, then 2 (provided they are in the ISR).

### 5.2 What the controller then writes

```
 before:  Leader 1   Epoch 0   Replicas 1,2,3   ISR {1,2,3}
 broker 1 fenced
 after:   Leader 2   Epoch 1   Replicas 1,2,3   ISR {2,3}
```

- **New leader id** (2).
- **Leader epoch + 1** (see section 7).
- **ISR shrinks** by the dead broker. Note that the ISR is the **current in-sync set**, not "who may be elected", but it is what election draws from.

### 5.3 What happens on the brokers

1. Broker 2 reads the metadata and **becomes leader** of the partition. It starts accepting produce and fetch requests, and starts a new epoch in its `leader-epoch-checkpoint` file.
2. Broker 3 **becomes a follower of broker 2**. It checks whether its log agrees with the new leader's and truncates if not (section 7), then fetches.
3. **Pending `acks=all` requests** that were waiting on the old leader are gone; those producers time out or get a `NOT_LEADER_OR_FOLLOWER` error and retry.
4. When broker 1 comes back it is **not** the leader. It rejoins as a follower, truncates divergent data, catches up, and re-enters the ISR.

### 5.4 Observed in note 3 (Lab 6) [observed, note 3]

The test partition had replicas `1,2,3`, leader 1. After `docker stop ep-kafka1`:

- Leader became **broker 2**, `Isr: 2,3`, visible about **17 s after the stop command was issued** (including the time the stop command itself took).
- `acks=all` writes via broker 2 **succeeded**.
- Broker 2's `leader-epoch-checkpoint` gained the line `epoch 1 starts at offset 15`, exactly the end of the old leader's log.
- After restart, broker 1 rejoined: ISR `1,2,3` in about 12 s, leader still broker 2, identical log size and `offsetLag 0`.
- A manual preferred election restored leader 1, giving **epoch 2 at offset 20**. No acknowledged record was lost.

## 6. Why the ISR makes it safe

The safety argument, in four steps:

1. A leader acknowledges an `acks=all` write only after **every member of the current ISR** has it (note 3, section 7.1), and only if the ISR had **at least `min.insync.replicas`** members.
2. So an acknowledged record exists on **all ISR members at that moment**, which is at least `min.insync.replicas` (2) brokers.
3. A clean election picks a replica **from the ISR**, and an ISR member is, by definition, caught up to the leader's committed data.
4. Therefore, if at least one of those brokers survives, **the new leader has every acknowledged record.**

```
 min ISR 2, ISR {A(leader), B}, record R acknowledged => R is on A and B
 A dies  -> elect B (in the ISR)    -> B has R           OK, no loss
 (ISR is now {B}, below min ISR 2: writes are REFUSED until another replica rejoins the ISR)
```

What this guarantee is **not**:

- **Not for `acks=1` or `acks=0`.** Those acknowledge before replication; the new leader may not have the record. (Note 3, section 7.)
- **Not for records that were never acknowledged.** A record that the old leader appended but did not replicate may vanish. The producer never saw an ack, so a retry covers it. That is the "maybe" zone (note 3, section 10).
- **Not against losing both copies at once.** Two brokers failing **simultaneously**, both holding the only two copies, can lose data. `min.insync.replicas - 1` simultaneous failures is the limit (note 3, section 8.1).
- **Not against a clean election when ISR members are all gone.** That is section 11.

A wrinkle to know: the guarantee is about the ISR **at the time of the write**. If the ISR shrinks and then the leader dies, the survivors still hold everything that was acknowledged, because acknowledgement required the ISR **at that time** to have the data. The danger comes from allowing a replica that was **not** in the ISR at that time to become leader. That is the entire reason unclean election is dangerous.

## 7. Epochs

### 7.1 The leader epoch

Every time a partition gets a new leader, its **leader epoch** increases by one. Each batch in the log records the epoch of the leader that wrote it (you saw `partitionLeaderEpoch` in the note 1 `kafka-dump-log.sh` output), and each replica keeps a `leader-epoch-checkpoint` file listing `epoch  first-offset-of-that-epoch` pairs. From note 3 [observed]:

```
0 0     epoch 0 began at offset 0
1 15    epoch 1 began at offset 15   (broker 2 became leader)
2 20    epoch 2 began at offset 20   (leadership moved back to broker 1)
```

The epoch is **the version number of "who was in charge"**.

### 7.2 What epochs are used for

| Use                                                              | How                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Truncating divergent follower logs**                           | A returning follower asks the new leader where the epoch it last followed ended, and cuts its log back to that offset. Records the new leader never had are removed, because they were never committed (they were beyond the high watermark when the old leader died) |
| **Fencing a stale leader (zombie)**                              | Requests and metadata carry the epoch. A broker that still thinks it leads at an older epoch is rejected, so two brokers cannot both accept writes for one partition                                                                                                  |
| **Detecting truncation on the consumer side**                    | A consumer's committed offset also stores the **leader epoch** (note 2, section 7.1). After a failover the consumer can notice that the log it was reading was truncated, instead of silently continuing past a gap                                                   |
| **Replacing the high-watermark-only truncation of old versions** | Truncating to "my high watermark" could lose or diverge data in some crash sequences; epochs make the cut point exact                                                                                                                                                 |

### 7.3 The one scenario you should be able to draw

```
 epoch 0: leader A, ISR {A,B}                        A log: 0..9    B log: 0..8   (HW = 9: B has 0..8)
 A dies.  B is elected, epoch 1, ISR {B}.
 B accepts new records:                              B log: 0..8, then 9' 10' ...  (epoch 1 starts at offset 9)

 A restarts. A's log has an offset 9 that B does NOT have (the old record 9, never committed).
   A asks B: "where did epoch 0 end?"  B says: offset 9.
   A truncates its log to 9 (drops its old record 9), then fetches B's 9' , 10', ...
 => the old, never-committed record 9 is gone; the cluster agrees on 9'.
```

Record 9 was never acknowledged under `acks=all` (B did not have it, so the old leader could not have received B's confirmation). Dropping it loses nothing that a client was promised.

### 7.4 Producers, ordering and duplicates across an election

- The producer's idempotence state (producer id, sequence numbers) is part of the log, so the **new leader knows which sequences it has seen**. A retried batch whose first attempt was replicated is recognized and not appended twice (note 3, section 13).
- Ordering of one key within one partition is preserved if the producer is idempotent, because a retried batch lands in its original position relative to later ones.
- A batch that was **appended on the old leader but not replicated** is **gone** after the election; its retry appends it fresh. A batch that was replicated but whose ack was lost is **detected as a duplicate** by sequence. Both are invisible to your code. HTTP-level retries are a different story (new `send()`, new sequence), so they can duplicate. The sink's `ON CONFLICT (event_id)` absorbs those.

## 8. Clients

What a producer and a consumer see during a clean failover:

```
 t0  leader's broker dies
 t0..t1  requests to it fail (connection reset) or hang (timeout): no leader reachable
 t1  controller has elected a new leader (after detection delay: ~0 s controlled, ~9 s crash)
 t2  client gets an error such as NOT_LEADER_OR_FOLLOWER / LEADER_NOT_AVAILABLE, or a connection failure,
     refreshes METADATA from any broker, learns the new leader
 t3  client resends to the new leader; success
```

- **Producer:** the errors are **retriable** and retried inside the library until the delivery timeout (note 3, section 10.1). A failover shorter than the delivery timeout is invisible to the caller except as **extra latency on that request**. A failover longer than the timeout surfaces as a send timeout. For EventPulse that means a 503 for the requests that were in flight, and the batcher's timeout and breaker decide how long they wait.
- **Consumer:** its fetch for that partition fails, it refreshes metadata and continues from its **position**. **A leader election does not cause a consumer group rebalance**; group membership is unchanged. The consumer's committed offsets are unaffected (the group coordinator for the group may itself have moved, because `__consumer_offsets` partitions have leaders too, and commits may fail briefly and be retried).
- **Order and visibility:** a consumer can only read up to the **high watermark**, so after the election it sees exactly the committed records and never data that is later truncated.
- **Duplicates:** a producer retry across an election can produce a duplicate only if the producer is **not idempotent** or the retry is at the application level.

Timings **[observed, note 3]**: planned stop of a follower, ISR updated about 5 s after the command started; crash of a follower, about 9 s (the session timeout); leader stop, new leader visible about 17 s after the command was issued, which includes the stop itself. Use these as the **order of magnitude** for test timeouts, not as constants.

## 9. Controlled vs crash

|                                   | **Controlled shutdown** (`docker stop`, SIGTERM)                                                                     | **Crash** (`docker kill`, power loss, OOM kill)                                |
| --------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Broker tells the controller first | Yes: asks to be shut down; the controller moves its leaderships to other ISR members **before** it exits             | No                                                                             |
| Leadership handover               | **Orderly**: new leaders exist before the old broker disappears, so the unavailability window per partition is small | **Reactive**: leaders are re-elected only after the controller notices         |
| Detection time                    | ~0                                                                                                                   | up to `broker.session.timeout.ms` (9 s here) **[observed, note 3: about 9 s]** |
| In-flight acked data              | Safe                                                                                                                 | Safe if acknowledged under `acks=all` and min ISR 2                            |
| In-flight un-acked data           | Usually completed                                                                                                    | May be lost (never acknowledged)                                               |
| Used for                          | Rolling restarts, upgrades                                                                                           | Failure experiments and real incidents                                         |

Rule for rolling restarts: restart **one broker at a time** and **wait until `--under-replicated-partitions` is empty** before the next one. Restarting a second broker while the first is still catching up can shrink an ISR below `min.insync.replicas` and stop writes (note 3, section 11.2).

## 10. Preferred

After a failover, leadership is on the replacement, which can leave brokers unevenly loaded (one broker leading many partitions). Kafka corrects it with **preferred leader election**:

- The **preferred leader** is the **first replica** in the partition's replica list (note 3, section 4).
- With `auto.leader.rebalance.enable=true`, the controller periodically (**every 300 s** here, `leader.imbalance.check.interval.seconds`) checks, per broker, whether the share of partitions it leads but should not (versus those it prefers) exceeds `leader.imbalance.per.broker.percentage` (default 10), and if so moves leadership back. **[reasoning]** for the exact 10% default; the 300 s and `true` are confirmed from this cluster's config.
- You can force it: `kafka-leader-election.sh --election-type preferred ...`. **[observed, note 3, Lab 6]**: leadership returned to broker 1 and the epoch went from 1 to 2.
- A preferred election is a **clean** election: it only moves leadership to a replica that is in the ISR and caught up, so it is safe. It does cause a **brief blip** (another epoch), so it is not free on a busy partition.

The only election that is not clean is the **unclean** one, and it never happens automatically unless you enable it.

## 11. No ISR

### 11.1 How the situation arises

For RF 3 and min ISR 2, here is the sequence that leaves **no live ISR member**:

```
 ISR {A,B,C}.  C is slow, falls out of the ISR            ISR {A,B}         (C is alive but stale)
 A dies. Clean election -> B                              ISR {B}           (B leads, but ISR 1 < min ISR 2: writes refused)
 B dies too.
   Live replicas: C only (stale).  ISR was {B}.  Nobody in the ISR is alive.  => partition OFFLINE
```

Writes stop here anyway, since the ISR was already below min ISR after the first failure. But an alternative sequence with `min.insync.replicas=1` would have let writes continue with a one-member ISR, and then the data **exists only on B**. If B dies, the only live replica is C, which lacks B's recent writes.

### 11.2 The two choices

| Option                                                               | What happens                                                            | Cost                                                                                                                                               |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Wait** (`unclean.leader.election.enable=false`, **default, ours**) | The partition stays **offline** until an ISR member (here B) comes back | **Unavailable** (reads and writes fail) for as long as B is gone. If B's disk is lost forever, the partition is stuck until an operator intervenes |
| **Unclean election** (`unclean.leader.election.enable=true`)         | The controller elects C immediately, although C is not in the ISR       | **Data loss** of everything B had that C did not, and consumer confusion (section 12)                                                              |

Nothing in Kafka can make this choice for you, because it is a business decision: _is it worse to be unavailable, or to lose records?_

### 11.3 A note on eligible leader replicas (Kafka 4.x)

`kafka-topics.sh --describe` on this cluster prints `Elr` and `LastKnownElr` columns, empty when healthy. These belong to the **eligible leader replicas** mechanism: a refinement that lets the controller remember which replicas were still fully caught up when the ISR dropped **below** `min.insync.replicas`, so it can elect one of them **safely** without resorting to a truly unclean election. **[reasoning]**: whether that feature is active depends on the cluster's feature level, which was not inspected here. You do not need it for this stage, and the plain rules above hold either way. If you want to know, check the cluster's `eligible.leader.replicas` feature level with `kafka-features.sh describe` later.

## 12. Unclean

### 12.1 Definition

> **Unclean leader election** is electing a replica that is **not in the ISR** as leader, because no ISR member is available.

It is controlled by `unclean.leader.election.enable` (broker default, per-topic override). It has been **false by default for years**, and this cluster sets it **false explicitly** at broker level (`STATIC_BROKER_CONFIG`, confirmed) and the topics show it in their configs.

### 12.2 What is lost, with offsets

```
 B is the leader and the only ISR member. It has acknowledged records up to offset 120 (min ISR 1, or acks=all with ISR {B}).
 C is alive but stale: it only copied up to offset 100.
 B dies.  UNCLEAN election: C becomes leader at offset 100 (epoch +1).

   records 101..120 : acknowledged to producers, now ONLY on B's disk, and B will not be leader again.
   C accepts new writes; the first one gets offset 101 (a DIFFERENT record from the old 101).
   B returns later: it truncates 101..120 (they conflict with C's log, as in section 7.3) -> destroyed for good.
```

Two kinds of damage:

1. **Silent data loss.** 20 records that clients were told were safe are permanently gone. The producers saw successful acks.
2. **Offset reuse with different content.** The offsets 101 and onward now name **different records** than before. Anything that remembered the old offsets is wrong.

### 12.3 What consumers see

- A consumer that had already read up to 120 (it could read below the old high watermark) and committed offset 121 has a **committed offset beyond the new log end** (about 101) after the election. When it resumes, the offset is **out of range**, and the reset policy decides: `earliest` jumps back to the log start and **reprocesses everything it still has**, `latest` jumps to the end and **skips** whatever it has not read, `none` fails (note 2, section 9).
- With leader-epoch-aware consumers, the committed offset carries the epoch, so the consumer can **detect that the log diverged** rather than blindly continue. **[reasoning]**: how your specific client surfaces that detection was not checked.
- A consumer that was **behind** (committed offset below 101) just continues and reads the replacement records; it never sees that anything changed.
- For the EventPulse sink this means: records that were in `events.raw` and **already stored** in Postgres are fine, but records that existed only in the lost 101..120 and **were acknowledged to the API's clients (202)** are **never** in Postgres and cannot be recovered from Kafka. The audit's check C ("acknowledged but not in the log") would report exactly this, which is the point of that check.

### 12.4 How the setting interacts with the others

| Combination                                     | Effect                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ----------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `unclean=false`, `min ISR 2`, `acks=all` (ours) | Never acknowledge fewer than 2 copies, never elect a stale replica. May be unavailable, never loses acknowledged data through a single failure                                                                                                |
| `unclean=true`, `min ISR 2`, `acks=all`         | Still never acknowledges fewer than 2 copies, but if both ISR members are lost and a stale replica is elected, data acknowledged before is lost. Less likely than with min ISR 1 because the data was on 2 machines, but possible if both die |
| `unclean=true`, `min ISR 1`                     | Most available, least safe: acknowledgements can rest on one copy, and a stale election discards them                                                                                                                                         |
| `unclean=false`, `min ISR 1`                    | Writes continue with one copy; if that one copy's broker dies, the partition is offline until it returns, and the data is safe on its disk (not lost, just unavailable)                                                                       |

### 12.5 The manual escape hatch

With `unclean.leader.election.enable=false`, an operator can still **force** an unclean election for one partition when a broker is permanently lost: `kafka-leader-election.sh --election-type unclean ...` **[reasoning]**: that this tool works in this cluster was not tried. This is a deliberate, one-off human decision to accept loss for one partition, which is far safer than turning the setting on for the whole cluster.

## 13. Choosing

| Workload                                                                                                           | Priority                                                                            | Setting                                          |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| **EventPulse `events.raw`** (events a client was told were accepted, then written to a ledger-like Postgres table) | **Do not lose acknowledged events**                                                 | `unclean=false`, `min ISR 2`, `acks=all`         |
| `events.dlq`                                                                                                       | Do not lose the evidence of failures                                                | Same                                             |
| `__consumer_offsets`                                                                                               | A lost commit means duplicate processing at worst; losing it entirely resets groups | Same (inherits the broker default)               |
| Lossy telemetry (click counts, debug logs) where staying online matters more                                       | Availability                                                                        | `unclean=true` could be reasonable. Not our case |

The same test for any topic: **"If the last good copy is gone, would I rather have an outage or a gap?"** For EventPulse the API can answer an outage honestly (503, the client retries) but cannot un-acknowledge a lost event, so an outage is the lesser harm.

## 14. Scenarios

All for `events.raw`: RF 3, min ISR 2, `acks=all`, idempotent, `unclean=false`, 6 partitions, 3 combined-mode nodes.

| Scenario                                                                       | Election                                                           | Writes                                                                                                                           | Data                                                           |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Leader of some partitions stopped gracefully**                               | Orderly, leaders move first                                        | Tiny blip on those partitions                                                                                                    | None lost                                                      |
| **Leader crashed**                                                             | After ~9 s detection, clean election to the next ISR member; ISR 2 | Writes to those partitions fail or time out for the detection + election window, then continue (ISR 2 is still >= min ISR)       | None lost                                                      |
| **A follower crashed**                                                         | None (not a leader)                                                | ISR shrinks to 2 after detection; `acks=all` still works                                                                         | None lost                                                      |
| **Two nodes crashed**                                                          | **No election possible** (no controller quorum)                    | `acks=all` times out; `acks=1` works against a surviving leader **[observed, note 3]**                                           | Acknowledged data safe on survivor; acks=1 data at risk        |
| **Active controller node crashed** (plus a leader there)                       | First a Raft controller election, then partition elections         | Slightly longer blip                                                                                                             | None lost                                                      |
| **Old leader partitioned from the cluster but alive** (zombie) **[reasoning]** | Controller elects a new leader; the old one is fenced by epoch     | The old leader cannot get `acks=all` confirmations or ISR changes approved, so it cannot acknowledge new writes under `acks=all` | None lost; its un-replicated tail is truncated when it rejoins |
| **Leader dies while a follower is out of the ISR**                             | Elects the only remaining ISR member; ISR size 1                   | **Refused** (ISR 1 < min ISR 2) until the third replica catches up and rejoins                                                   | None lost                                                      |
| **All ISR members die, a stale replica alive**                                 | **Offline**, no election (unclean off)                             | Unavailable                                                                                                                      | Data safe on the dead brokers' disks                           |
| Same, but `unclean=true`                                                       | Stale replica elected                                              | Writable again                                                                                                                   | **Acknowledged data lost**, consumer offsets confused          |

Everything in the "None lost" column assumes data was acknowledged with `acks=all` and min ISR 2.

| Decision                         | Value                                                                                                                                                      | Reason                                             |

### 15.2 Build checklist

**Step 1: S2-F1**

- [ ] Confirm `unclean.leader.election.enable=false`, `min.insync.replicas=2`, `default.replication.factor=3` and the same for the internal topics, with `kafka-configs.sh --describe --all`.
- [ ] The producer retries `NOT_LEADER_OR_FOLLOWER` and leader-election errors internally. Do not catch and hand-roll a retry for them; set the delivery timeout instead.
- [ ] The API contract's wording for 202: acknowledged by **all in-sync replicas, at least 2**, which is what makes it survive a single leader failure.

**Step 5: S2-F5**

- [ ] The batcher's failure path expects **transient** errors during a failover, not only outages. A failover is short; the circuit breaker should **not** open on a single failed batch (`KAFKA_BREAKER_FAILURES` > 1) or one failover will cause a needless 503 burst.
- [ ] In broker-kill e2e tests, assert: some requests may 503 during the failover window; none return 202 and then vanish; after the window the API returns 202s again without a restart; the audit's checks C and E show no loss.
- [ ] Budget the detection delay: about 9 s after `docker kill`, a few seconds after `docker stop` **[observed, note 3]**, plus election and metadata refresh.
- [ ] Check what the Node client returns for a failover error and for the delivery timeout, and put them in `isKafkaUnavailable`.

**Steps 2 and 3: consumers**

- [ ] A leader election is **not** a group rebalance. Do not count it in `ep_consumer_rebalances_total`, and do not expect `isStale()` to trip because of it.
- [ ] Commits can fail briefly during a failover (a coordinator or `__consumer_offsets` partition leader moved). That is the "commit failure after a successful DB write" case: duplicates, never loss.
- [ ] If you ever enable unclean election in an experiment, expect out-of-range committed offsets and apply `CONSUMER_FROM_BEGINNING` knowingly.

**Step 7: S2-F7**

- [ ] Check C ("Kafka offsets delta >= acknowledged") is the check that detects lost acknowledged records. Under the safe settings it must pass after any single failure; under `acks=1` or unclean election it should be able to fail. Keep both cases as test fixtures.

## 18. Common confusions

1. **"Brokers vote on the new leader."** The active controller decides and records it in the metadata log. (Voting is only for choosing the controller itself.)
2. **"Any follower can become leader."** Only an ISR member, unless unclean election is enabled.
3. **"Election picks the most up-to-date replica."** It picks the **first live ISR member in the replica-list order**. Every ISR member is considered caught up.
4. **"Leader election makes consumers rebalance."** No. Group membership is unchanged. Fetches are retried against the new leader.
5. **"A failover loses data."** Not acknowledged `acks=all` data with min ISR 2. It can lose records that were never acknowledged.
6. **"Unclean election is just a faster election."** It is a different decision: it elects a replica that may be missing committed data.
7. **"If unclean is off, the partition can never lose data."** It cannot lose it **through election**. Both replicas dying at once, `acks=1`, or a disk loss still can.
8. **"Offline partition means data is lost."** No. The data sits on the dead broker's disk. It is unavailable, not gone.
9. **"The old leader comes back and takes over again."** It returns as a follower and truncates. It regains leadership only through a preferred election.
10. **"Two controller elections and partition elections are the same."** They are two layers; the second needs the first to be working.
11. **"One failover produces one error and that is it."** The producer retries inside the library; the caller sees extra latency or one timeout.
12. **"Restarting two brokers one after another is always safe."** Only if the first has fully rejoined the ISR before the second goes down.
13. **"Turning unclean on is fine because we have min ISR 2."** It reduces the chance, but a stale election after both ISR members die still loses acknowledged data.

<summary>Short answers</summary>

1. The active controller (a KRaft quorum node); the decision is a record in the cluster metadata log (leader, epoch, ISR).
2. Broker failure (fenced), controlled shutdown, preferred-leader rebalance, topic creation, reassignment, operator-requested election.
3. Choose the first replica in the partition's replica list that is in the ISR and alive and not shutting down.
4. An acknowledged record is on every ISR member (at least 2 brokers). The new leader is an ISR member, so it has the record, as long as one of those brokers survives.
5. Records never acknowledged (appended on the old leader but not replicated), and anything acknowledged with `acks=1` or `0`.
6. A counter that increments per leader change, stamped on batches and stored as `epoch -> first offset`. A returning old leader asks the new leader where its epoch ended and truncates to that point; stale-epoch requests are fenced.
7. A: 0..9, B: 0..8 (B elected, epoch 1, writes 9', 10'). A returns, learns epoch 0 ended at 9, truncates its 9, fetches 9', 10'.
8. Retriable errors or timeouts, then a metadata refresh and a retry to the new leader; internally the client library retries, so the caller sees extra latency or, past the delivery timeout, a timeout.
9. No. A fetch error, metadata refresh and retry. Commits may fail briefly if the coordinator's `__consumer_offsets` partition leader moved.
10. No goodbye on a kill; the controller waits for the heartbeat session to expire: `broker.session.timeout.ms` (9 s). Observed about 9 s vs about 5 s.
11. ISR {A,B,C} -> C falls out -> A dies, B elected, ISR {B} -> B dies. Only stale C is alive.
12. Wait (safe, unavailable until an ISR member returns, stuck if its disk is gone) or unclean election (available immediately, loses what the stale replica lacks).
13. Electing a replica outside the ISR. It can destroy acknowledged records that existed only on the lost ISR members, and it causes offset reuse with different content.
14. Offset 121 is beyond the new log end, so it is out of range (or detected as truncation by epoch). The reset policy decides: earliest reprocesses, latest skips, none errors.
15. Acknowledged events must not be silently lost. With unclean off, an unavailable partition makes the API return 503 and clients retry; no acknowledged event disappears.
16. The three voters need a majority (2) to have an active controller, and only the controller elects leaders and changes ISRs. Observed in note 3 Lab 7: `acks=all` timed out and admin commands hung.

## 20. Cheat sheet

### Glossary

| Term                           | Meaning                                                                        |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Leader election                | Choosing the new leader of a partition when the old one is gone or being moved |
| Active controller              | The KRaft quorum leader that makes election and ISR decisions                  |
| Clean election                 | New leader chosen from the ISR                                                 |
| Unclean election               | New leader chosen from outside the ISR (may lose committed data)               |
| Preferred leader               | First replica in the replica list                                              |
| Leader epoch                   | Counter of leader changes per partition; versions "who was in charge"          |
| Fencing                        | Rejecting requests from a replica with a stale epoch                           |
| Truncation                     | A follower dropping log records the new leader does not have                   |
| Controlled shutdown            | Broker hands off leadership before stopping                                    |
| Offline partition              | No eligible leader; unavailable for reads and writes                           |
| Eligible leader replicas (ELR) | Kafka 4.x tracking that helps safe election when the ISR drops below min ISR   |

### The election in one picture

```
leader dies
   -> controller (after detection: ~0 s controlled, ~9 s crash)
   -> first live replica from the ISR, in replica-list order
   -> epoch + 1, ISR shrinks, written to the metadata log
   -> brokers apply; followers truncate to the new leader; clients refresh metadata and retry
   no ISR member alive?
        unclean=false (ours): partition offline, wait
        unclean=true:         elect stale replica, lose data
```
