## 1. TL;DR

- A retry without a delay is an attack on your own dependency. A retry with a **fixed** delay synchronises all clients. A retry with **exponential** delay plus **jitter** spreads the load and gives the dependency room to recover.
- **Exponential backoff:** delay = `base * 2^(attempt-1)`, capped at `max`. The doubling gives quick first retries (for blips) and rapidly decreasing pressure (for outages).
- **Jitter** is not decoration. Without it, 1,000 clients that failed together all retry together, over and over. With it, retries decorrelate.
- **Bounded attempts** means _two_ limits: a maximum **count** and a maximum **elapsed time**. Either alone is insufficient. The effective bound is whichever fires first.
- Retries **multiply across layers** (3 layers of 3 retries = 27 calls). Budget retries at **one** layer where possible, and propagate a deadline.
- In a Kafka consumer the retry loop must **finish before the group gives up on you** (`max.poll`/session/rebalance timeouts). That constraint is why a long wait becomes a **pause or a retry topic** (note 3), not a longer `sleep`.
- Respect a server's `Retry-After`. It is better information than any formula.

> **One-line version:** _Wait longer each time, add randomness, stop after N tries or T seconds, and never let the loop outlive its caller._

## 2. The mental model: a crowd at a door

A shop opens after a power cut. A thousand customers were waiting outside.

| Behavior                                                | Result                                                                                                              |
| ------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Everyone pushes the door continuously (no backoff)      | The shop cannot even unlock the door; the push _is_ the outage                                                      |
| Everyone returns exactly every 10 s (fixed delay)       | Every 10 s a thousand people arrive in the same second; the shop is crushed at those instants and idle between them |
| Everyone waits 10 s, then 20 s, then 40 s (exponential) | Load falls over time but the crowd remains synchronised: the same thousand arrive together at 10, 30, 70...         |
| Exponential **plus random offsets** (jitter)            | Arrivals spread across the interval; the shop sees a smooth trickle it can serve                                    |

Backoff is **politeness over time**; jitter is **politeness across clients**.

## 3. Why wait at all

Retrying immediately is justified in exactly one situation: the failure was a **race that has already resolved** (a serialization failure `40001`, a deadlock victim, a leader that just moved). For everything else, an immediate retry:

1. **Probably fails again.** The cause (restart, overload, failover) lasts longer than a round trip.
2. **Adds load** to a dependency that is already struggling, lengthening the outage (a feedback loop).
3. **Burns the retry budget** in microseconds, leaving nothing for the real recovery period.
4. **Wastes the caller's resources** (threads, connections, CPU).

A delay buys three things: time for the dependency to recover, time for load to drop, and a chance for operators to intervene before the budget ends.

## 4. Backoff strategies

| Strategy                 | Formula (attempt n = 1, 2, 3 ...) | Sequence with base 100 ms                  | Use                                |
| ------------------------ | --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------- |
| **Immediate**            | 0                                 | 0, 0, 0                                    | Only for one retry of a known race |
| **Fixed**                | `d`                               | 100, 100, 100                              | Polling, trivial cases             |
| **Linear**               | `base * n`                        | 100, 200, 300                              | Gentle ramps; rarely best          |
| **Exponential**          | `min(max, base * 2^(n-1))`        | 100, 200, 400, 800, 1600                   | Default for dependency failures    |
| **Exponential, cap 5 s** | same with `max=5000`              | 100, 200, 400, 800, 1600, 3200, 5000, 5000 | Prevents absurd waits              |
| **Fibonacci**            | 100, 100, 200, 300, 500           | Slower growth than doubling                | Niche                              |
| **Decorrelated**         | `min(max, random(base, prev*3))`  | Random walk                                | Used with jitter (section 6)       |

### 4.1 Why exponential

- **Fast first retries** recover from blips with almost no added latency.
- **Rapidly falling retry rate** protects a dependency in a long outage: after 10 doublings the rate per client dropped about a thousand times.
- **Bounded total** thanks to the cap: delays never exceed `max`, so the retry duration is predictable.

### 4.2 The total time of a policy

For base `b`, cap `m`, `N` retries the total sleep (without jitter) is the sum of the capped series. Example `b=100 ms, m=5000 ms, N=3`: 100 + 200 + 400 = **700 ms** of waiting. With `N=8`: 100+200+400+800+1600+3200+5000+5000 = **16.3 s**. The number to remember is not N, it is the **total wall-clock budget**. A policy of "3 retries" that sums to 700 ms cannot survive a 20 s database restart, no matter how elegant the maths.

### 4.3 The cap matters

Without `max`, attempt 15 waits 100 ms \* 2^14 = 27 minutes. The cap turns an exponential into "exponential, then constant", which is what you want for a long outage: steady, low-rate polling for recovery.

## 5. Why jitter: the thundering herd

Imagine 1,000 consumers, each with one partition, all hitting one database that restarts at t = 0.

**Without jitter** (base 1 s, doubling):

```
retry waves at t =  1,  3,  7, 15, 31, 63 ...  seconds
requests in each wave = 1,000 simultaneously
```

Each wave is a spike the database must absorb _at the moment it is starting up_. Between waves it is idle. The average load looks fine; the **peak** is lethal. Any bucket or pool sized for steady load fails on the spike, the retries fail, and the next wave is the same size: the outage self-sustains. This is the **thundering herd** (or synchronised retry storm).

**With jitter** each client's wave is spread over the interval. The 1,000 requests of the first wave are uniformly spread over, say, 0.5 to 1.0 s, i.e. about 2,000 requests per second _spread_ rather than 1,000 in one instant. Peak load drops by one to two orders of magnitude, and recovery completes.

The AWS Architecture Blog analysis of exponential backoff and jitter (a well-known reference; read it for the simulation charts) found that adding jitter reduces both total completion time and total calls; the "full jitter" variant was the best performer in their model of contending clients. Treat that as the empirical reason jitter is the default, not as a number to quote.

### 5.1 Jitter also fixes correlated start times

Synchronisation does not require a shared clock. It arises whenever many clients share a _trigger_: a restart, a deploy, a broker failover, a cron schedule on the hour. After any shared trigger, deterministic backoff keeps them aligned forever.

### 5.2 Jitter and Kafka consumers

In Kafka the herd is real: a consumer group of 30 pods and a Postgres failover is a 30-client herd; add the 6-partition fan-out and multiple chunks in flight and you reach hundreds of simultaneous attempts. Jitter is cheap insurance.

## 6. The jitter variants

Let `cap(n) = min(max, base * 2^(n-1))`.

| Variant                 | Delay                              | Range                     | Properties                                                                 |
| ----------------------- | ---------------------------------- | ------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **None**                | `cap(n)`                           | exact                     | Herd                                                                       |
| **Full jitter**         | `random(0, cap(n))`                | 0 to cap                  | Best spread; can pick near-zero delays, so the _minimum_ is not guaranteed |
| **Equal jitter**        | `cap(n)/2 + random(0, cap(n)/2)`   | cap/2 to cap              | Guarantees at least half the backoff; less spread than full                |
| **Decorrelated jitter** | `min(max, random(base, prev * 3))` | grows from previous delay | No explicit exponent; each client random-walks upward                      |
| **Additive**            | `cap(n) + random(0, j)`            | cap to cap+j              | Simple; weaker at breaking big herds                                       |

How to choose:

- **Full jitter** is the best default for pure load spreading against a shared dependency.
- **Equal jitter** is preferable when you want a **floor** (never retry sooner than `cap/2`) for something expensive such as a connection storm against a restarting database.
- **Decorrelated** helps when you want independent random walks with no dependency on attempt number (useful when attempts are interleaved across tasks).

### 6.1 Numeric comparison

`base=100 ms, max=5000 ms`, attempts 1 to 5 (cap = 100, 200, 400, 800, 1600):

| Attempt | None | Full jitter range | Equal jitter range |
| ------- | ---- | ----------------- | ------------------ |
| 1       | 100  | 0 to 100          | 50 to 100          |
| 2       | 200  | 0 to 200          | 100 to 200         |
| 3       | 400  | 0 to 400          | 200 to 400         |
| 4       | 800  | 0 to 800          | 400 to 800         |
| 5       | 1600 | 0 to 1600         | 800 to 1600        |

Expected total (mean) over 5 attempts: none 3,100 ms; equal jitter ~2,325 ms (0.75 of cap each); full jitter ~1,550 ms (0.5 of cap each). **Jitter shortens the average wait**, so the _time budget_ (section 7) must be set against the worst case, not the mean.

## 7. Bounding attempts: count, time, and the budget

An unbounded retry loop is a bug (note 1, section 3.1). Bounding has two independent dimensions.

| Bound                           | What it protects                                                                                     | Failure if used alone                                                                                         |
| ------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Max attempts**                | Stops retrying a record that keeps failing for its own reasons                                       | A long backoff × a few attempts can run for minutes; a short backoff × many attempts can burn out in a second |
| **Max elapsed time (deadline)** | Keeps the loop inside what its caller will tolerate (poll interval, HTTP timeout, rebalance timeout) | Short, cheap retries may be abandoned too early; no limit on wasted attempts                                  |

Use both and stop at **whichever comes first**.

### 7.1 Per-record bound vs per-dependency window

EventPulse separates two budgets **[observed in `config.ts`]**:

| Setting                          | Default | Meaning                                                                                              |
| -------------------------------- | ------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `SINK_MAX_RETRIES`               | 3       | Retries of **one isolated bad record** before it becomes a dead letter                               |
| `SINK_RETRY_BASE_MS`             | 100     | First backoff                                                                                        |
| `SINK_RETRY_MAX_MS`              | 5,000   | Backoff cap                                                                                          |
| `SINK_TRANSIENT_RETRY_WINDOW_MS` | 20,000  | How long to keep retrying a **down dependency** inside one handler call before pausing the partition |

The distinction is important: a down database is bounded by **time** (a window, then pause and hold the work), while a permanently bad record is bounded by **count** (a small N, then quarantine). Using one number for both would either quarantine good records during an outage or retry bad records for a whole window.

### 7.2 What happens when the bound is reached

The bound is not "the end"; it is a **decision point**:

| Situation                                                         | On exhaustion                                                                                                    |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Bad record, count exhausted                                       | Dead-letter with attempts recorded (note 4)                                                                      |
| Dependency down, window exhausted                                 | Pause the partition, keep the offset, let the poll loop and heartbeat run; retry later (note 3 for long outages) |
| Transient but cumulative budget (per-key or per-minute) exhausted | Park on a retry topic with a `not_before` header                                                                 |

The worst design is "retry N times and then log and continue", which is **silent data loss** with a log line.

### 7.3 Counting attempts durably

If the retry loop is purely in memory, a restart resets the counter, and a poison record can bounce between restarts indefinitely. For bounds that must hold across restarts or across consumers, the attempt count must travel **with the message** (a header such as `retry.attempt`, note 3) or be stored durably.

## 8. Retry budgets across layers

Retries compound. A request that passes through layers each retrying independently multiplies:

```
client (3 attempts) x gateway (3) x service (3) x database driver (3) = 81 calls for one user action
```

Real example from this repo's neighbouring notes ([producer note 5](../../../stage-2/learning/producer/5.%20kafka-producer-retries-and-timeouts.md), section 11): the producer library, the micro-batcher, the circuit breaker and the HTTP clients each have their own timers; the shortest wins, but all of them may keep working.

Practices:

1. **Retry at one layer**, ideally the one closest to the failure that has the most context, and make the others fail fast.
2. **Propagate a deadline** (an absolute "give up at T") instead of re-starting full budgets at each hop.
3. **Retry budgets (token buckets):** allow retries only up to a percentage (for example 10%) of the base request rate. When the dependency fails everything, the budget runs dry and retries stop, preventing amplification. This idea is described in Google's SRE book (client-side throttling); read the chapter on handling overload.
4. **Do not retry what a lower layer already retried** unless the failure type is different.
5. **Idempotency keys** are required wherever retries are layered, since you cannot know which layer's retry succeeded.

## 9. The consumer constraint: do not outlive the group

A Kafka consumer in a group must keep proving it is alive.

| Timer                                                                                                                           | Role                   | Consequence of breaching it               |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Heartbeat (separate thread / loop)                                                                                              | Keeps session alive    | Session timeout evicts the member         |
| `max.poll.interval.ms` (in the Java client; in kafkajs-compatible clients the equivalent is the rebalance / processing timeout) | Max time between polls | Evicted, rebalance, partitions reassigned |
| Rebalance timeout                                                                                                               | Max time to rejoin     | Evicted                                   |

See [consumer note 2 (rebalances)](../../../stage-2/learning/consumer/2.%20kafka-consumer-rebalances.md) for the mechanics. The implication for retries: **a retry loop that sleeps inside the handler is stealing time from these timers.** Hence the config check in `config.ts` **[observed]**:

```ts
// from the validation in config.ts (paraphrased)
if (retry.baseMs > retry.maxMs)
  throw new Error("SINK_RETRY_BASE_MS must be <= SINK_RETRY_MAX_MS");
// SINK_TRANSIENT_RETRY_WINDOW_MS + SINK_RETRY_MAX_MS must be < CONSUMER_REBALANCE_TIMEOUT_MS
```

The window plus the largest single sleep must fit **inside** the rebalance timeout, otherwise one retry loop can get the consumer kicked out of the group, which triggers a rebalance, which re-delivers the same chunk to someone else, which also retries, which... The fix for waits longer than the budget is **not a bigger timeout**; it is to **stop blocking**: `pause()` the partition so polling and heartbeats continue (see [consumer note 5, backpressure](../../../stage-2/learning/consumer/5.%20kafka-consumer-backpressure.md)) or **move the message to a retry topic** (note 3).

## 10. Server hints: Retry-After and rate limits

When the dependency tells you how long to wait, use it.

| Source                                                    | Hint                             | Rule                                  |
| --------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| HTTP `Retry-After` (seconds or a date) on 429 and 503     | Exact minimum delay              | Wait at least that; add jitter on top |
| Rate-limit headers (`X-RateLimit-Reset`, vendor-specific) | Window reset                     | Align to reset plus jitter            |
| gRPC `RetryInfo`                                          | Structured delay                 | Same                                  |
| Kafka `throttle_time_ms` in responses                     | Broker is throttling this client | Clients honor it automatically        |

Rule: `delay = max(server_hint, backoff_with_jitter)`. Ignoring the hint wastes retries; following it blindly without jitter re-creates the herd at the hint boundary (everyone retries exactly at second 30).

EventPulse's own ingest API sends `Retry-After` on 503 (producer note 5 mentions it among the load-control tools). The same discipline should be expected of clients of that API.

## 11. Retry amplification and retry storms

A **retry storm** is a positive feedback loop: failure causes retries; retries cause more load; more load causes more failure.

```
dependency slows down -> timeouts -> every client retries -> offered load doubles -> slower -> more timeouts
```

Mechanisms to break the loop:

| Mechanism                       | How it helps                     |
| ------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Exponential backoff             | Retry rate falls over time       |
| Jitter                          | Removes spikes                   |
| Retry budget                    | Caps total extra load            |
| Circuit breaker                 | Stops calls entirely while open  |
| Load shedding at the dependency | Fail fast instead of queueing    |
| Fail fast at the caller         | Do not wait for a doomed call    |
| Bounded queues                  | Memory cannot grow without limit |

The sink-side version: all six partitions hit the same Postgres; with 3 sink pods each draining chunks of 1,000 rows, a restart produces dozens of simultaneous connection attempts per second. Backoff with jitter plus pausing the partition (rather than spinning) keeps that bounded.

## 12. Circuit breakers and pausing

Backoff decides _how long to wait between attempts of one operation_. A **circuit breaker** decides _whether to attempt at all_.

```
CLOSED (normal) --N failures--> OPEN (reject immediately for cooldown) --cooldown--> HALF-OPEN (let a probe through)
      ^                                                                                  |
      +------------------------------- probe succeeds <---------------------------------+
                                       probe fails -> back to OPEN
```

In the ingest API the breaker is `KAFKA_BREAKER_FAILURES=3`, `KAFKA_BREAKER_COOLDOWN_MS=5000` (producer note 5). A consumer's analogue is **pausing the partition**: stop fetching, keep the group membership alive (poll and heartbeat continue), and **resume** after a delay. In effect, the pause is the consumer's open circuit.

Backoff + breaker + budget together are the standard resilience triad. Backoff alone is not enough when 1,000 callers all back off _independently_; the breaker is the shared signal.

## 13. Idempotency, deadlines and cancellation

### 13.1 Idempotency

Every retry is a potential duplicate (see [delivery-semantics note 2](../delivery-semantics/2.%20why-duplicates-are-inevitable-two-generals.md)). Before adding retries to an operation, prove one of:

1. The operation is naturally idempotent.
2. It carries an idempotency key stored and checked by the receiver.
3. It is wrapped in a transaction whose outcome can be checked.

EventPulse's sink satisfies (1) via `ON CONFLICT (event_id) DO NOTHING`.

### 13.2 Deadlines instead of timeouts

A **timeout** is relative to now ("5 s from this call"); a **deadline** is absolute ("by 12:00:05"). Passing a deadline downward stops every layer from granting itself a fresh 5 s; the sum of waits can never exceed the original promise. Use the remaining time to decide whether another attempt is even worth starting: if remaining time is less than the minimum possible success latency, **fail immediately**.

### 13.3 Cancellation

A giving-up caller should cancel the work in flight where the protocol allows, otherwise you get "zombie writes" (producer note 5, section 10): the caller reported failure, the work completed anyway. Where cancellation is impossible, idempotency is the only protection.

## 14. EventPulse walkthrough: reading backoffMs

The function **[observed]** in [`errors.ts`](../../../../services/consumer-sink/src/sink/errors.ts):

```ts
export function backoffMs(
  attempt: number,
  baseMs: number,
  maxMs: number,
  rand: () => number = Math.random,
): number {
  const cap = Math.min(maxMs, baseMs * 2 ** Math.max(0, attempt - 1));
  return Math.round(cap / 2 + rand() * (cap / 2));
}
```

This is **equal jitter** (section 6): cap doubles per attempt up to `maxMs`; the delay is uniformly random in `[cap/2, cap]`. Note three design details:

1. `attempt` starts at 1 and `Math.max(0, attempt - 1)` makes attempt 0 behave like attempt 1 rather than producing `base/2`.
2. The `rand` argument is injectable, so tests can pass `() => 0` (the minimum), `() => 1` (the maximum) and a fixed value: deterministic unit tests of a random function.
3. The floor of `cap/2` guarantees the dependency a minimum rest, which suits "database restarting".

Values with the defaults (base 100, max 5000):

| Attempt | cap           | Delay range  |
| ------- | ------------- | ------------ |
| 1       | 100           | 50 to 100    |
| 2       | 200           | 100 to 200   |
| 3       | 400           | 200 to 400   |
| 4       | 800           | 400 to 800   |
| 5       | 1600          | 800 to 1600  |
| 6       | 3200          | 1600 to 3200 |
| 7       | 5000 (capped) | 2500 to 5000 |
| 8+      | 5000          | 2500 to 5000 |

Worst-case cumulative wait through attempt 7: 100 + 200 + 400 + 800 + 1600 + 3200 + 5000 = 11.3 s; through attempt 8: 16.3 s; attempt 9: 21.3 s, which is just past the 20 s `SINK_TRANSIENT_RETRY_WINDOW_MS`. So with default settings a down database is retried roughly **8 times within the window** and then the partition is paused. This is a calculation from the formula and the configured defaults, not a measurement.

The per-record bound uses `SINK_MAX_RETRIES=3`: an isolated bad row waits at most 100 + 200 + 400 = 0.7 s before becoming a dead letter, so a poison record costs well under a second rather than a window.

## 15. Choosing numbers

A practical procedure:

1. **Measure the failure you want to survive.** How long does a Postgres restart take? A failover? A deploy? (Stage 2 measured broker failover at 9 to 17 s in [Kafka note 3](../../../stage-2/learning/Kafka/3.%20kafka-replication-isr-acks.md).)
2. **Set the window** slightly above the p95 of that duration, **below** the consumer's rebalance timeout minus the max single backoff.
3. **Pick the base** near the time of a normal blip (tens to hundreds of ms).
4. **Pick the cap** so that polling a long outage is cheap but recovery is detected promptly (seconds, not minutes).
5. **Choose the jitter** (equal for a floor, full for max spread).
6. **Pick the per-record count** low (2 to 5): a message that fails in isolation is almost never rescued by attempt 6.
7. **Validate relationships in config** (base < cap; window + cap < rebalance timeout) so a bad edit fails at startup.
8. **Test the whole budget** in a fault-injection run, not just the formula.

| Mistake                              | Symptom                                            |
| ------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Window longer than rebalance timeout | Consumer evicted during outages                    |
| Cap too low                          | Constant hammering of a dead dependency            |
| Cap too high                         | Slow detection of recovery                         |
| Count too high for poison            | Seconds wasted per bad record                      |
| No jitter                            | Periodic spikes visible on the dependency's graphs |

## 16. Testing a retry policy

1. **Pure function tests** with injected randomness: min, max, midpoint; attempt 0, 1, large attempts (no overflow: `2 ** 1000` becomes Infinity; `Math.min` with the cap handles it, but test it).
2. **Bounds:** with a fake clock and a fake sleep, assert the loop stops at count N or at time T, whichever is first.
3. **Distribution test:** run 10,000 draws; assert all fall in range and the mean is near the expected value.
4. **Synchronisation test:** simulate 1,000 clients and assert the peak requests-per-second in any 100 ms bucket is far below 1,000.
5. **Config invariants:** each forbidden combination throws at startup.
6. **Interaction tests:** an outage longer than the window must pause, not dead-letter ([note 1](1.%20transient-vs-permanent-failures.md)).

Using fake `sleep` and `now` (EventPulse's `HandlerDeps` takes `sleep`, `now` and `rand` for this) turns minutes of waiting into milliseconds of test.

## 17. Observability

| Metric                                            | Insight                                                    |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Retry attempts per outcome (success at attempt k) | Shows how often attempt 1 fails and how deep recovery goes |
| Sleep time total / histogram                      | Cost in latency                                            |
| Retries exhausted (by class)                      | The rate of "gave up" events                               |
| Partition pause count and duration                | Dependency outage footprint                                |
| Retry rate / base request rate                    | The amplification factor; alert when above the budget      |
| Dependency error rate vs retry rate               | Verifies backoff reduces load rather than increasing it    |

A log line per retry attempt is a mistake at scale (the poison loop wrote one long line per retry, [observation](../../../stage-2/s2-f4-poison-before-fix.md)); log the first and the last, count the rest.

## 18. EventPulse decisions

| Decision          | Choice                                            | Why                                                 |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Backoff shape     | Exponential, capped                               | Fast first retries, bounded later                   |
| Jitter            | Equal jitter (`[cap/2, cap]`)                     | Guarantees a minimum rest for a restarting database |
| Per-record bound  | `SINK_MAX_RETRIES` = 3                            | Poison costs under a second                         |
| Dependency bound  | 20 s window, then pause                           | Fits inside the rebalance timeout                   |
| Config validation | Base <= cap; window + cap < rebalance timeout     | Bad edits fail at startup                           |
| Randomness        | Injected `rand`, `sleep`, `now`                   | Deterministic tests                                 |
| Beyond the window | Pause partition (and later park on a retry topic) | Never block the poll loop                           |

## 19. Common confusions

1. **"Exponential backoff is enough."** Without jitter clients stay synchronised.
2. **"Jitter makes retries random so it is unpredictable."** The _range_ is deterministic and testable; only the exact value varies.
3. **"More retries is safer."** More retries at multiple layers is how outages become longer.
4. **"Set max attempts to 5 and we are bounded."** 5 attempts with a 10 min cap is 50 minutes.
5. **"A bigger timeout fixes long outages."** It makes the consumer fall out of the group; pause or park instead.
6. **"Retry-After is optional."** It is the most accurate delay you will get.
7. **"The retry loop belongs in every layer."** Pick one layer; the others fail fast.
8. **"Full jitter is always best."** It can choose a near-zero delay; use equal jitter if you need a floor.
9. **"Attempt counters in memory are fine."** They reset on restart; durable bounds travel with the message.

## 21. Glossary and cheat sheet

| Term                               | Meaning                                        |
| ---------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| Backoff                            | Waiting between attempts                       |
| Exponential backoff                | Delay doubles each attempt, up to a cap        |
| Jitter                             | Randomisation of the delay                     |
| Equal / full / decorrelated jitter | Variants of the random range                   |
| Thundering herd                    | Many clients acting in sync                    |
| Retry budget                       | Cap on retries as a fraction of normal traffic |
| Deadline                           | Absolute time by which work must complete      |
| Circuit breaker                    | Stops calls after repeated failures            |
| Retry storm                        | Retries causing further failures               |

```
delay(n) = min(max, base * 2^(n-1))                 exponential
equal    = delay/2 + rand * delay/2                  floor + spread
full     = rand * delay                              maximum spread
stop at  = attempts >= N   OR   elapsed >= T         both bounds
honor    = max(Retry-After, delay)
constraint: window + max single sleep < rebalance timeout
```

Rules: retry at one layer; bound by count and time; jitter always; honor server hints; pause instead of sleeping long; counters that matter travel with the message; budget with the worst case, not the mean.
